# Phase 19: Advanced Materials

## Goals and Purpose

Phase 19 completes our material system by implementing the Disney BRDF - the industry standard for physically-based rendering that unifies metals, dielectrics, and everything in between into a single, artist-friendly model. We're solving the problem of realistic material appearance: real surfaces aren't perfectly smooth or rough, perfectly metallic or dielectric, but exist on continuums. The Disney BRDF provides intuitive parameters (roughness, metallic) that map to complex physical phenomena while maintaining energy conservation and reciprocity.

The critical test here is that our materials exhibit the full range of real-world appearances: the sharp reflections of polished metal, the soft specularity of brushed aluminum, the complex Fresnel of dielectrics, and the subtle transitions between these extremes. The implementation requires sophisticated importance sampling through Visible Normal Distribution Function (VNDF) sampling, which dramatically reduces variance by sampling microfacets based on their visibility from the viewing direction.

## What We're Building

### The Disney BRDF System

**Unified Material Model** combines multiple physical phenomena into a single evaluation and sampling framework. Instead of separate code paths for metals and dielectrics, the Disney model uses the metallic parameter to blend between them. At metallic=0, we get dielectric behavior with colored diffuse and achromatic specular. At metallic=1, we get conductor behavior with no diffuse and colored specular. Values between create alloys and oxidized metals.

**GGX Microfacet Distribution** models surface roughness as a statistical distribution of microscopic facets. The GGX distribution (also called Trowbridge-Reitz) produces the long-tailed highlight falloff observed in real materials, from the tight highlights of smooth surfaces to the broad lobes of rough ones. The roughness parameter controls the width of this distribution, with roughness² (alpha) used in the actual mathematics for perceptual linearity.

**Fresnel Equations** determine how much light reflects versus transmits at material interfaces based on viewing angle and refractive indices. For dielectrics, we use Schlick's approximation which accurately captures the increase in reflectance at grazing angles. For metals, the Fresnel term becomes the base color, creating the characteristic colored reflections of copper or gold.

**VNDF Sampling** revolutionizes importance sampling by considering masking and shadowing in the sample generation itself. Traditional sampling generates microfacets then checks if they're visible, wasting samples on occluded facets. VNDF directly samples from the distribution of visible normals, dramatically improving convergence for rough surfaces viewed at grazing angles.

### Material Parameter Processing

```glsl
MaterialProperties process_material_properties(MaterialProperties props) {
  // Precompute derived values for efficiency
  
  // Alpha (physical roughness)
  props.alpha = props.roughness * props.roughness;
  
  // Fresnel F0 based on metallic parameter
  if (props.metallic < 0.01) {
    // Dielectric: achromatic specular
    float f0_dielectric = (1.0 - props.ior) / (1.0 + props.ior);
```
