## Corrections

1. **H3 drift: the result does not support the stated conclusion.** In the twin, the ball sits at the origin, so its local frame is the global frame and the local-frame decision is never exercised. "wall" mode is a projection of the point back onto the surface, not a change of frame. "model" renormalization failed only because the twin's `ballNormal` gives the normal at radius R, which does not match a point that has merely been rescaled onto the hyperboloid. recheck/drift_check2.ts uses a normal computed at the renormalized point and the general far root. With those, generic hyperboloid renormalization is as good as the wall projection: worst per-segment error |⟨g,g⟩+1| is 4e-12 vs 6e-12 at R=2 and 3e-7 vs 4e-7 at R=4, with 0/2000 failures. Without correction it fails, reproducing the finding qualitatively (R=4: 17/2000 NaN; furnace RENORM=none and RENORM=model crash at R=4 [checked-num recheck/furnace.ts]). The quoted sequence 1e-13 → 5e-4 → 6e-2 came from a deleted trace and cannot be reproduced. Simplest rule supported: renormalize every vertex onto the model, one generic line, and evaluate normals at the actual point. In float64, both treatments degrade at R=6 (1e-2 per segment) and fail at R=8 (drift_check2.ts), so segment length, not the frame, limits precision. Float32 prediction (only [math], ε32·e^{2t}): trouble already at segments of length about 4–5.
2. **The Gram–Schmidt claim is not reproducible, and its stated mechanism is wrong.** No script for the "fixed threshold fails about 4 units out" claim survives. recheck/gs_check.ts shows that far from the origin the Minkowski length² of the projected candidates grows. At s=8 the second-longest is ≥ 2.6e3 over 2000 random (p,n). This contradicts "every candidate shrinks". Picking the longest candidate is fine, but the claim should be marked unverified.
3. **Missed site: in S3, a ray leaving a surface always comes back to its start at t = 2π, on the other face.** γ(2π) = x for every geodesic [math]. So "geodesics that hit nothing score 0" holds for camera rays in free space, but not for a ray spawned at a surface: such a ray hits its own surface at exactly t = 2π unless something else is hit first. The twin's `hitDisk` only tries the roots t0 and t0+π, so it never reports 2π. recheck/s3_cone_domain.ts: a ray leaving a lone disk gets no hit for 2000 of 2000 rays, even with tmax = 2π+1e-9. The design has to say whether the far limit is (0, 2π] with an analytic self-return, and must face that the return point lands exactly on the spawn point, which numerically is a coin flip between hit and miss. No twin tested this, because the irradiance receiver is a bare point and the furnace is closed.
4. **Missed site: the S3 cone formula holds only for R < d < π − R.** If the ball contains −x (d > π − R), every direction hits it, and S(R)/S(d) > 1 [checked-num s3_cone_domain.ts: d=2.8 R=0.5 and d=2.5 R=2.0 give 1.0000 of directions hitting]. The repo's `sqrt(max(0, 1 − sin²))` would not produce a NaN. It would return 1 − cos θmax = sin²θmax > 1, a wrong pdf with no warning. The S3 sphere sampler therefore needs a "whole sphere of directions" branch. The inside test must also become d < R instead of S(d)² ≤ S(R)². No sphere configuration in sanity.ts or irradiance.ts reached this regime.
5. **A simpler S3 arc option was missed, and it uses facts that already exist.** `lighting_sample(LightQuery q, …)` and `lighting_pdf(LightQuery q, …, Hit light_hit)` both receive the query [checked-code src/compiler/generate/features/lighting.ts:221,228]. The MIS weight replays the stored query [checked-code src/components/transport/combiner.ts:39]. `q.two_sided == false` is the receiver's claim that it scatters nothing from below n [checked-code src/glsl/core/structs.glsl:77-82]. So: when the query is one-sided and n ≠ 0, take the arc that leaves into n's hemisphere with probability 1. The discarded arc's integrand is zero by that claim, so this is unbiased [math], and each sample equals the summed-both-arcs estimator B [math]: no variance penalty and no contract change. When the query is two-sided or a medium event, pick an arc with probability ½. This is the notes' option (c) restricted to where it is licensed. The notes call (c) "biased for transmission and media", which is true only without that restriction. The S3 two-cone sphere sampler gets no such shortcut when both cones straddle the horizon.
6. **The variance figure needs context.** The 67× is relative to estimator B, which has almost no variance in that case. One random arc gives var ≈ 2σ² + μ² [math]. The one-arc estimator (se 0.00090) is still better than cosine sampling (se 0.0011) on the same case [checked-num s3_one_arc.out.txt].
7. **The counts are wrong.** irradiance.ts runs 17 configurations, not 75 cases. Its output holds 64 numeric z values: 42 against a closed form and 22 pairwise, plus 3 NaN rows for the exactly zero case. "75 comparisons" does not match the file.
8. **The MIS-weight explanation is misleading.** By symmetry A(2π−d) = A(d), so the length of the arc traveled does not change A. What it changes is which face is seen and the arrival direction. The arrival velocity is −direction_to(y,x) only for the short arc; for the long arc it is +direction_to(y,x). The notes' "cos_y needs no new seam" is right only with that sign rule. The pdf side can choose the arc from light_hit.t > π (lighting_pdf already receives the Hit [checked-code lighting.ts:228]). The Euclidean `quad_light_pdf` computes cos_l from wi at p (src/components/lights/quad/quad.glsl:37). That body must be replaced in curved spaces, which the file's "METRIC EXEMPTION" comment already anticipates (quad.glsl:10-11).
9. **The furnace pt rows test nothing.** In a closed furnace, pt has zero variance for any A(r). Only the NEE and MIS rows test the area-to-solid-angle conversion.
10. Minor:
    - Antipodal caustic h=0.01: B has z = 2.66 (heavy tail; pointlight.out.txt), so "matched by all estimators" is a little strong.
    - "H3 ball off-axis: every estimator gives 0.040314": C gives 0.0403126.
    - The H2xR citation is labeled [checked-code], but it is a survey document (curved-literature.md lines 23 and 275, themselves [V-src]). Label it from the survey.

## Verdict

The core math is correct and reproduces: A(r) = S(r)², the cone and disk-edge formulas for R < d < π−R, the S3 two-arc structure, the point-light law and both equiangular forms. The estimator-equality numerics hold. Three design conclusions need changes:
- The H3 drift result supports per-vertex renormalization with consistent normals, not the local-frame decision specifically.
- S3 needs the 2π self-return and the "ball contains −x" branch, both missed.
- For opaque surfaces, the S3 LightSample question has a simpler answer than either option: choose the arc deterministically from the existing LightQuery.
