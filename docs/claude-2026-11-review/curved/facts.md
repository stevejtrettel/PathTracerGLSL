
# bypass-inventory

SUMMARY: I built a site-by-site inventory of the Euclidean assumptions in src/ that would be wrong with 4-vector points in H³ or S³. It has 220 rows plus 5 rows for the seam occupant's own definitions. A row is one function, one block, or for GRIN and similarity.ts one whole file. Of the 220, 146 are in the owner's first subset, 61 sit behind features that will be compile errors at first, and 13 are features nobody has decided yet (equiangular, softbeam, the normal AOV, expression materials that read p).

By category: PLACEMENT 37 (29 in subset), CHART 32 (25 behind compile errors), FRAME 27 (22), FLOW 24 (17), DISTANCE 24 (20), FAR 18 (12), DENSITY 18 (10), CAMERA 15 (11), CONNECTIONS 12 (8). I added two categories: TYPE 12 (10), for literal vec3 where a Point or Direction belongs, and NUMERIC 1 (fp_uncertainty).

The method: one complete grep (536 hits, saved), the source read with line numbers, and generated shaders dumped for representative suite scenes, written only to the scratchpad. Numeric checks are in checks.ts and bitident.ts, and count.sh produces the counts from the notes file.

The biggest structural facts:
1. Placement is folded into world-space shape parameters as Euclidean similarities. Curved spaces need a 4×4 isometry per object and have no scaling.
2. The single Point typedef is used for world points, for shape and light struct rows, and for local vec3 shape bodies all at once.
3. Five transport sites use the ray's starting direction where they need the direction at the hit.
4. Light samplers need new per-space bodies, because routing E³ through a distance-based seam would change the bits.
5. S³ has two geodesics joining two points, which breaks pt ≡ pt-nee unless NEE handles both arcs.

- **Direction at the hit point (FLOW, subset)** [checked-code]: Four transport sites use the direction the ray started with, where they need the direction at the hit or event: the side test intersection.ts:1694 `ambient_dot(ray.direction, hit.ng, hit.p)`, the null-interface continuation pt.ts:410 `ray_spawn(hit, s.ray.direction)`, `wo = -s.ray.direction` at pt.ts:420, and `ms.exit_dir = ray.direction` at analytic.glsl:32 (also delta_tracking.glsl:111 and grin.glsl:325, both behind compile errors). The seam's ambient_geodesic returns only the point, so the simplest fix is one more seam function returning the direction at t (in E³ it returns d, identical bits), or a direction stored in Hit. Separately, three generated spots compute a straight-line point: intersection.ts:559 and :763/:765, and the march at geometry/index.ts:234. Those can be respelled through ambient_geodesic.
- **Light samplers mix DISTANCE, CONNECTIONS and DENSITY in Euclidean code** [checked-num (bitident.ts) + checked-code]: In the subset kinds (point, quad, disk, sphere) every sampler and pdf mirror uses `d = q - p; d2 = dot(d,d); wi = d*inversesqrt(d2)`, measures the emitter cosine as `dot(l.normal, -wi)` (it needs the direction the geodesic arrives with at q), and uses d2 as the density: point.glsl:12-18, quad.glsl:15-40, disk.glsl:17-45, sphere.glsl:21-70. In f32, routing E³ through a distance-based seam (r = sqrt(d2), then r*r) changes the result in 49% of random cases, so E³ cannot be refactored onto such a seam and stay bit-identical. The files already say 'curved spaces get new bodies'. That points to per-space bodies for point, disk and sphere lights.
- **Density and cone laws confirmed numerically** [checked-num (checks.ts #6, #7)]: Sphere-light cone half-angle: sin θmax = sinh R / sinh d in H³ and sin R / sin d in S³. A brute-force tangent search matched both formulas to 5 digits at four (d, R) pairs. The current Euclidean asin(R/d) is off by 3× at d=3, R=0.5 in H³. The solid-angle density then follows as A(d) = sinh² d (H³) and sin² d (S³).
- **FAR: MAX_DIST is used as a real geodesic length** [checked-num (checks.ts #1, #2) + checked-code]: MAX_DIST (math.glsl:11) is the starting nearest hit (intersection.ts:1682) and the medium segment end (pt.ts:331). It is also the light distance for the environment sampler (environment.ts:81, sampler_cdf.glsl:78) and the directional light (directional.glsl:22), which light.glsl:20 and light_medium.glsl:14 then pass to ambient_geodesic. In f32, cosh overflows at t = 89.4, and a point stored at distance t breaks the hyperboloid constraint ⟨p,p⟩ = −1 by 1e-3 at t=5, 1.67 at t=9 and 22 at t=10. The sky lookup takes a world direction: kernel.glsl:13 `environment_radiance(s.ray.direction)`, the signature at environment.ts:95/251, and equirect_uv. H³ needs the ideal endpoint of the ray (origin and direction) instead.
- **S³ has two geodesics joining two points (CONNECTIONS)** [math]: A light at q reaches p along the short arc and along the long arc (length 2π − d), and both have density 1/sin² d. A short-arc NEE sampler has pdf 0 for a BSDF ray that meets the light at t > π. pt-nee drops emission that BSDF rays find at samplable emitters, so pt-nee would lose the long-arc light and pt ≡ pt-nee would fail. This would need new transport machinery: either NEE samples both arcs, or pt-nee counts BSDF hits at t > π. H³ and H²×R have one geodesic between any two points. The affected sites are ambient_direction_to (euclidean.glsl:31-33, light.glsl:22) and every sampler's wi.
- **FRAME: H³ and S³ differ** [checked-num (bitident.ts) + checked-code]: In S³ a tangent vector is an R⁴ vector and the metric is the R⁴ dot product, so raw dot() and normalize() on tangent vectors at one point are correct once Direction is vec4. In H³ they are wrong: a unit tangent vector at distance 2 has Euclidean norm 5.2257. Of the 22 subset FRAME rows, 10 are only wrong in H³: microfacet.glsl:26, lambert.glsl:23, checker.glsl:34, mirror.glsl:27, dielectric.glsl:31 and :39, hg.glsl:13 and :33, rayleigh.glsl:11 and :28. The other 12 need a tangent basis or a per-space normal in both spaces: build_basis (cross is vec3-only in GLSL), sphere-light basis, primitive normals, the generated 4-tap SDF normal at index.ts:337-348, and the constant-env sampler's world-axis wi at environment.ts:80.
- **PLACEMENT is the largest subset category (29 rows)** [checked-code]: Constant placements are folded into world-space shape parameters (index.ts:356-452 derivedFold and foldPlacementIntoParameters). Driven and patterned shapes use the placement.glsl quaternion and scale helpers. Generated arms add mat3 and `/ s` placement code: intersection.ts:515-531, 601-656, 723-754, 1145. The plane has its own fold (plane.ts:39), similarity.ts is entirely similarity math, authoring/flatten.ts composes group transforms, and the Validator has scale rules. H³ and S³ have isometries only, no scaling. The E³ fold has no curved equivalent, so every curved object would go through the placed-arm path with a mat4 isometry.
- **One Point typedef serves three roles** [checked-code]: Point and Direction are #defined in core (structs.glsl:4-5), not by the ambient occupant. schema.ts:28-29 maps every 'point' or 'direction' row in shape and light structs to Point/Direction, so a global Point=vec4 also turns Sphere.center, Quad.corner and QuadLight.corner into vec4. Meanwhile the shape bodies take `vec3 p`. With each object at the origin of its own frame, curved shapes are canonical and have no center rows. So curved spaces need either their own small canonical shape list or new per-space row machinery. There are also 12 TYPE sites: signatures such as scene_region_at(vec3 p) and scene_material_properties(…, vec3 p, …), `LightQuery(p, vec3(0.0), …)` at pt.ts:137 and kernel.ts:35, and light constructors emitted with formatVec3 at lighting.ts:410-424.
- **Euclidean SDFs: safe in H³ through the Klein chart, not in S³ through the gnomonic chart** [checked-num (checks.ts #4, #5)]: For 200,000 random pairs in the Klein ball, hyperbolic distance was at least the Euclidean chart distance (smallest ratio 1.0016). So a Euclidean SDF evaluated in an object's Klein coordinates is a safe sphere-tracing step in H³. In the gnomonic chart of S³ every pair with |x| < 3 had a spherical distance shorter than the chart distance (ratio down to 0.12), so the same trick is unsafe there. This matters for menger, apollonian, bottle, knob, torus and defineSDF. The SDF conservativeness gate (boundCheck.ts) and the marchBound facts are Euclidean too. Supporting arbitrary SDFs in S³ would need new machinery.
- **Small hyperbolic distances need a stable formula** [checked-num (checks.ts #3)]: acosh(f32 cosh d) has 100% relative error at d = 1e-4 and 2.3% at d = 1e-3. asinh of the transverse part stays accurate to about 5e-8. Any H³ DISTANCE seam and sphere SDF must avoid acosh(−⟨p,q⟩) near zero. fp_uncertainty (math.glsl:54-56) also ignores p.w.
- **CAMERA: the pose is position and target; equirect and cylindrical cost no more than fisheye** [checked-code]: The cameras build world rays from vec3 u_cameraPosition and a forward/right/up basis computed on the CPU (camera.ts:86-130, basis.ts:30-55). The Validator (:846), withPose, OrbitControls and KeyboardControls (App layer) all speak position and target. A curved pose would be an isometry uniform. pinhole.glsl:11-17 and fisheye.glsl:29-38 change only in the final world assembly. Equirect (:18-27) and cylindrical (:21-27) also work entirely in the eye's tangent space, so keeping them as compile errors saves nothing structural. Thinlens (focus point on a straight line, lens offset) and orthographic ('parallel rays') do need new geometry.
- **Already correct in curved space** [checked-code + math]: Homogeneous media are arc-length only: analytic.glsl Beer–Lambert, distance sampling, transmittance, and exit_p through ambient_geodesic. The same holds for delta tracking segments, ray_spawn, the hit.p fills (arc length survives isometries), the classification probes, light.glsl's light point, aim and cosine through the seam, media.glsl's re-aim, the microfacet local math, Fresnel, MIS, the sampler, accumulators, tonemaps and color. Point-light power 4πI is the same in any space. Power-based light selection uses Euclidean areas, but it only affects variance because the pmf is mirrored in lighting_pdf. The quad light's area, however, enters its pdf, so it is a correctness issue (quad.glsl:30/40, quad.ts:13-15).
- **Quad light and quad geometry in curved space** [math (disk not checked numerically)]: A geodesic quadrilateral has no closed-form uniform-area sampler, and in-quad tests and uv are chart questions (quad.glsl:9-41, lights/quad/quad.glsl:15). A geodesic disk does have one: area 2π(cosh r − 1) in H³ and 2π(1 − cos r) in S³, sampled by inverting the area. The sphere light's cone law is confirmed. So the cheapest area lights for the subset are disk and sphere. A quad light would need a chart Jacobian, which is extra machinery.
- **Where H²×R differs** [math + survey (thurston-port.md, geometry-accel.md R1)]: Flow splits into an H² part and a vertical part (thurston's version gives NaN for vertical rays). Distance is sqrt(d_{H²}² + Δz²), and geodesics are unique. Density depends on direction: one transverse Jacobi field grows like sinh(λt)/λ and the other like t, so a density helper must take a direction, not just r. The ideal boundary is not a round sphere, so the sky must be parametrized by an H² ideal point and a slope. The isometry group Isom(H²)×Isom(R) excludes rotations that tilt the vertical, so authored quaternions are too general. No chart makes geodesics straight, so slab tests and BVHs do not port.
- **Only one gate exists for unsupported features** [checked-code]: The only space gate today is Validator.ts:56-57 ('Ambient space … not yet supported'), and AmbientDescriptor is just {type, glsl} (ambient/index.ts:13-23). The compile errors for X-marked features, and any record of which features each space implements, have no home yet. ambient_parallel_transport is defined but never called.
- OPEN: Undecided features with Euclidean code. Equiangular medium NEE (T9-T12 and the pt.ts:327 hook), softbeam lights (L23-L25, plus the cone gate at materials.ts:941), the normal AOV (normal.glsl:8-10), and GLSL-expression material rows that read world `p` (values.ts:75). Should each be a compile error in curved spaces, or part of the first subset?
- OPEN: In S³, should NEE sample both arcs (two shadow rays per light sample), or should pt-nee count BSDF hits beyond π? The alternative is to declare long-arc light a truncation and accept that pt ≠ pt-nee there. A choice is needed before estimator-equality witnesses can pass in S³.
- OPEN: In H³ with an ambient medium, rays run to infinity. Is the far limit a declared scene radius (a new bias-ledger entry), given that f32 points lose the hyperboloid constraint past about t = 5-9?
- OPEN: Are Euclidean-authored SDF shapes (menger, apollonian, knob, bottle, torus, defineSDF) in the first curved subset? In H³ they could be evaluated in the object's Klein chart, which is a safe step. S³ has no such chart, the gnomonic one is unsafe, so S³ would need a different chart or exclusion. Or should curved spaces start with true SDFs only (ball, half-space, tube)?
- OPEN: Quad lights and quad geometry in curved space: drop them from the first subset in favor of disk and sphere, or define them in a chart and pay for a Jacobian?
- OPEN: Groups and authored scale: authoring/flatten.ts composes similarities with scale, and the Validator accepts scale > 0. In H³/S³, should scale be a compile error, or should it be absorbed into length rows (a sphere's radius, say), which changes the shape's meaning?

The reason is that the conformal factor 2/(1+|x|^2) is at least 1 there and the hemisphere is geodesically convex [math]. A chart used for SDF stepping does not need straight geodesics, so S³ uses the same mechanism as H³ with a different chart formula, as long as the march bound fits inside the hemisphere (radius < π/2).
- Missed S³ case for the sphere light: when p is within R of the antipode of the light's center (d > π-R), every direction from p meets the ball by t=π. The reason is that all geodesics from p pass through -p, which lies inside the ball. - F7/M7 two-arc analysis is incomplete and overstates the cost [math, not checked numerically]. Their long-arc light is part of the measurement, so NEE must take that arc too or the omission becomes a declared truncation. A point light also refocuses at its antipode, where the 1/sin² d density diverges, so expect fireflies there. (b) The long arc reaches q with the negated velocity: the short arc arrives with -sin d p + cos d v, the long arc with sin d p - cos d v. So for a one-sided quad or disk light exactly one arc lands on the emitting side, and NEE can take that arc at no extra sample cost, with density 1/sin² d either way. (c) A sphere light appears as two equal caps, around w and around -w, so a 50/50 choice between them is a few lines. # capability-tracking

SUMMARY: The codebase records no per-space support fact today: AMBIENT_SPACES has only euclidean, and its descriptor is {type, glsl} (components/ambient/index.ts:14-23) [checked-code]. For other axes, support facts are recorded in seven different ways. (P1) Membership in a registry, checked at 14 separate gates in Validator.ts. (P2) A support list naming another family's keys: sensor.colorModels, color.wavelengthSamplers, accumulator.statistics, spectrum-kind roles. This is the closest precedent. (P3) Optional facts or flags on descriptors, read by generic rules (treeBounds, deltaQuery, provides, bounds, material capabilities). (P4) Rules in the Validator that test an occupant's name (CF-7), mostly pairwise. (P5) A per-mode limitations function (spectralLimitations). (P6) Implicit support through typedefs (Spectrum vec3/vec4), shown by a test that enumerates the materials. (P7) The seam linkage check in merge.ts.

There are 64 occupants in 18 registries [checked-num: matrix.mts, which imports the real registries]. 56 have a glsl field. 8 estimator occupants emit their code from TypeScript. The compiler also imports 12 component GLSL files directly, with no descriptor.

Color support needs no per-component fact because radiometry is pointwise in wavelength. Space does not work that way. A point light's falloff is 1/r², 1/sinh²r or 1/sin²r [math], so "compiles in H³" is not evidence of support. Materials and phase functions are the exception, since they work in a tangent frame.

Mitsuba 3 [web] instantiates every plugin for every enabled variant (configure.py loops over one global list). It has no per-plugin support list. Unsupported combinations either throw at run time (blackbody: "Not implemented for non-spectral modes") or silently degrade (the polarizer returns 0.5·transmittance). pbrt-v4 [web] uses closed TaggedPointer type lists, and every type must implement every method. Its GPU path rejects options with scattered ErrorExit calls, and in one case it warns and substitutes the volpath integrator. Neither has a support matrix, and both allow late or silent outcomes.

Recommended (design 1): in the 7 space-dependent families, each descriptor keys its GLSL by space. "Supports X" then simply means "has a body for X". Code that is the same in every space is listed under each space, pointing to the same file, with no wildcard: a wildcard would claim support for future spaces, which is a guess. The film-side families stay unchanged. One spaceGate helper is called at the existing registry gates; it covers each light kind and its backing primitive. Its message reads "<family> '<key>' has no <space> implementation (implemented in: …)" [checked-num: design1-mock.mts]. Anything without a descriptor body goes in one rows table, spaceLimitations, in the style of spectralLimitations. That includes meshes, instancing, table dispatch (and the Planner's size-based default at Planner.ts:558), the light tree, tracking media, GRIN, equiangular and the environment. One vitest toMatchFileSnapshot test prints the matrix, so a stale matrix fails CI.

Estimated size is about 200 lines plus 38 one-line descriptor edits. Euclidean output stays identical if the origins are kept; the digest can confirm that. Design 2 (one registry per space) touches about 140 lookup lines and splits each occupant's facts across folders. Design 3 (typedef polymorphism only) would allow wrong images: it guesses. The CF-7 pairwise rules should stay where they are. The matrix can also print the existing P2/P3 facts read-only, as an option.

The closed forms are all correct in float64:
- flow matches RK4 integration in conformal charts;
- the exp-map Jacobian matches r², sinh²r, sin²r and r·sinh(r·sin b)/sin b;
- the closed-form transport matches step-by-step projection transport;
- the arrival velocity equals minus the reverse geodesic's start direction;
- makeTranslation lies in SO+(3,1) and SO(4), and its column i is the transported basis vector e_i.

S3 has no precision problem in float32 anywhere, because every coordinate is at most 1.

H3 in float32 world coordinates is the main finding. The rounding error of a stored point grows like e^{2R}, where R is the distance from the world origin: 1e-6 at R=2, 5e-5 at R=4, 2e-3 at R=6. As a result:
- ray_spawn needs eps of about 2.5e-6·cosh²R: 3e-5 at R=2, 1e-3 at R=4, 0.1 at R=6.
- Shading-frame orthonormality degrades the same way: 5e-4 at R=4, 3e-2 at R=6.
- Intersecting each object in its own local frame only gains about 3x, because the ray state itself lives in world coordinates.
- Composing isometries in float32 far out fails. Re-orthonormalizing after each step (reduceIso) makes it worse, which matches thurston-rebuild's own comment.

The simplest design is therefore a declared scene radius of about 2–3 around the world origin, checked at compile time, with compositions done in float64 on the CPU.

Other findings: microfacet_from_local's GLSL normalize() is wrong in H3. Light samplers would need curved bodies, and keeping Euclidean output bit-identical then means two code paths. In S3 each light connection is two geodesics.

# primitive-bodies

SUMMARY: I checked what each of the 11 geometry primitives should mean in H³ and S³, with every formula verified against brute force in float64 TypeScript scripts; nothing was compiled as GLSL or rendered.

Seven primitives have a natural meaning and an exact or conservative field:
- sphere becomes a geodesic ball;
- plane becomes a totally geodesic plane (in S³, a closed great sphere);
- disk becomes a geodesic disk in such a plane;
- quad becomes the plane cut by two slabs, which is a rectangle in the Klein chart;
- box becomes three slabs, a box in the Klein chart;
- cylinder becomes a capped tube around a geodesic;
- torus becomes a tube around a circle.

Ball, plane, disk, quad, box and cylinder have closed-form intersections. All of them matched brute force with 0 hit/miss mismatches and |Δt| ≤ 1e-11. The torus is marched, as in E³, and has an exact distance and an exact normal.

Menger, apollonian, bottle, knob and scene-local defineSDF fields have no meaning in curved space. I recommend making them compile errors in the first subset. A Euclidean field evaluated in a chart and divided by the chart's Lipschitz bound is provably never an overestimate. I record that as a possible later route, not a recommendation.

Marching along the exact geodesic converges, with step counts close to Euclidean. Finite-difference normals taken along a tangent frame work.

Two measured findings bear on decisions already made:
1. **Float32 precision of the object-frame choice.** Owner decision 9(1) put each object in its own frame because that was expected to keep float32 precision best. For an H³ ball with the ray starting at the frame origin, folding the ball's center into the ray's frame was much more accurate than moving the ray into the object's frame. At distance 6 and radius 0.1, the object frame got 3.4% of near-edge rays wrong and the ray frame 0%. This is one case only, not a recommendation.
2. **The torus march bound differs per space.** The Euclidean bound is invalid in S³: 13655 of 40000 points of the solid torus fall outside it. Corrected per-space formulas are in the notes.

Beyond those, the main new machinery is per-space GLSL bodies and parameter rows per primitive, plus per-space validation facts. A per-space body map whose presence declares support could also serve as the "who implements what" table the owner asked for.

Side finding: the comment at src/components/geometry/index.ts:336 says the 4-tap normal stencil has the "same order of accuracy" as the central 6-tap. It does not: it is first order, with 1.5e-3 rad error against 2.2e-6 at the same step size, in E³ too.

Everything else, including the GLSL sketches, the labels on every claim, the timings and the H²×R differences, is in the notes file. The scripts (each says what it checks) are in the same folder.

- **Sphere: a geodesic ball with an exact distance and a closed-form hit** [checked-num]: Distance: H³ asinh(length(p.xyz)) − R; S³ atan(length(p.xyz), p.w) − R. Near the surface in float32 (R = 0.01) these are about 40× more accurate in H³ and about 1700× in S³ than the acosh(w) and acos(w) forms (ball.ts §3).

H³ hit: a quadratic in e^t. It avoids cancellation through two identities. First, the discriminant is sinh²R − |P×V|², where P and V are the spatial parts of the ray's point and direction, and |P×V|² = sinh²(distance from O to the ray). Second, (p.w+v.w)(p.w−v.w) = 1 + |P×V|².

S³ hit: entry at φ − α and exit at φ + α (mod 2π), with α = atan2(√(sin²R − |P×V|²), cos R).

Brute force: 0 hit/miss mismatches in 600 rays per space. In float32 the robust H³ form matches a float64 evaluation of the same rounded inputs, so the formula adds nothing. The naive form gets 24.7% of grazing rays wrong at R = 0.01, distance 3; the robust form 0.13%. GLSL sketches are in notes §1.
- **Float32 limit of the hyperboloid ray in the object's frame (bears on decision 9(1))** [checked-num]: In the object's frame, rays near the edge of the ball degrade with the distance d from ray origin to object.

| R | d | rays wrong |
|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | 6 | 3.4% |
| 0.01 | 6 | 25% |
| any | 9 | about 50% |

The direction's components are about cosh d in size, so the error grows roughly as ε·e^{2d}/4 [math, rough].

The same formula with the ball's center folded into the ray-origin frame (ray starting exactly at the frame origin) did much better.

| R | d | object frame wrong | ray frame wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | 6 | 3.4% | 0% |
| 0.1 | 8 | 46% | 0.23% |
| 0.01 | 6 | 29% | 0.15% |

(frame_precision.ts.) This covers one case only. Secondary rays starting far from the frame origin, and oriented shapes, were not tested. It is a measurement for the owner to weigh, not a recommendation to change the decision.
- **Plane: a totally geodesic plane, i.e. a linear hyperplane in R⁴** [checked-num]: Distance: asinh(p.y) in H³, asin(p.y) in S³. Both are exact; brute-force differences were 2.6e-4 in H³ (limited by the sampling) and 6e-8 in S³.

H³ hit: e^{2t} = (W − P)/(W + P), a hit only when that exceeds 1, with P = ⟨p,N⟩ and W = ⟨v,N⟩. That is one log and no atanh. S³ hit: t = mod(atan(−P, W), π). Every ray meets every S³ plane within π.

Brute force: 0 mismatches, |Δt| ≤ 9e-15.

Normal offset can fold into a single 4-vector, N = (cosh s·n, sinh s) in H³ or (cos s·n, −sin s) in S³ [math].

In GLSL write sign(x)*asinh(abs(x)). GLSL's asinh is built from the formula log(x + √(x² + 1)) [mem]. Emulated in float32, that gives −7.6246 at x = −1000 (exact −7.6009) and −Infinity at x = −1e4 (asinh_neg.ts).
- **Disk and quad: pieces of a plane; the quad is a Klein/gnomonic rectangle** [checked-num]: Disk test: |h.xyz|² ≤ sinh²ρ in H³; atan(|h.xyz|, h.w) ≤ ρ in S³. Its area 2π(cosh ρ − 1) in H³ and 2π(1 − cos ρ) in S³ matched Monte Carlo.

A Euclidean parallelogram (corner, edge1, edge2) has no curved counterpart that keeps its meaning. The natural quad is the plane cut by two slabs with half-widths a and b. Its test is |h.x| ≤ tanh(a)·h.w and |h.z| ≤ tanh(b)·h.w (tan in S³). Its edges are geodesic.

Corner angle: cos α = sinh a·sinh b in H³ and −sin a·sin b in S³; measured equal to 6 digits. Area by Gauss–Bonnet matched Monte Carlo. H³ needs sinh a·sinh b < 1.

The quad's parameter rows therefore differ from E³ (a, b instead of corner and edges); that choice is the owner's. Quad lights would need non-uniform sampling with an exact pdf (1 − |k|²)^{3/2}/(4 tanh a tanh b) in H³; that is a lights topic.
- **Box: three slabs (Klein box). Cylinder: a capped tube about a geodesic** [checked-num]: Box:
- Faces are planes with normals (±cosh h·eᵢ, sinh h) in H³.
- Distance: the maximum over the six faces of asinh/asin(⟨p,N⟩). It never overestimated and is exact inside.
- H³ hit: the slab method in τ = tanh t, monotone in t; 0 mismatches.
- S³ hit: candidate roots (each face's roots, kept when inside the other four faces); 0 mismatches.
- H³ needs Σ tanh² hᵢ < 1.
- Corners are not right angles: cos(dihedral) = sinh hᵢ sinh hⱼ.

Cylinder:
- Distance from the axis: asinh(|p.xz|) in H³, atan(|p.xz|, |p.yw|) in S³. This equals thurston-rebuild's acosh(√(w² − y²)) form but is stable.
- Cap term: asinh(|p.y| cosh h − p.w sinh h) in H³ (asin and cos/sin in S³).
- The maximum of the two never overestimated and is exact inside. The E³ exact corner term does not carry over.
- Hit by candidate roots: the tube is a quadratic in e^{2t} (H³) or a double-angle equation with 4 roots per 2π (S³), and the caps are plane roots. 0 mismatches, |Δt| ≤ 2e-15.
- S³ needs r and h below π/2.
- Float32 robustness of the tube quadratic was not tested.
- **Torus: exact distance in a stable form, but its march bound differs per space** [checked-num]: The nearest core-circle point is c = (sinh R·ê, 0, cosh R). Distance: 2·asinh(|p − c|_Minkowski / 2) − r in H³, 2·asin(|p − c| / 2) − r in S³. Error against brute force was ≤ 3e-7.

This chord form beats the acosh/acos form for thin tubes in float32. At r = 0.01, H³ error is 6.8e-5·r against 4.6e-3·r; S³ 7.8e-6·r against 1.7e-3·r. The exact normal points from c toward the surface.

The E³ march bound (cylinder of radius R + r, half-height r; torus.ts:24-30) behaves differently:
- In H³ it is valid but loose. The tight half-height is asinh(sinh r / cosh R).
- In S³ it is invalid: 13655 of 40000 points of the solid torus fall outside (R = 1, r = 0.3). The correct half-height is asin(sin r / cos R), and a bound of this shape exists only when R + r < π/2 (torus_bound.ts).

So bound parameters are per space.
- **Menger, apollonian, bottle, knob, defineSDF: compile errors first; chart embedding recorded as an option** [checked-num]: A Euclidean field evaluated on chart coordinates and divided by a bound L on the chart's stretch is provably never an overestimate [math]. The measured stretch ranges inside radius ρ:

| Chart | Stretch range |
|---|---|
| H³ Klein | [1/cosh²ρ, 1] |
| H³ exponential map | [ρ/sinh ρ, 1] |
| H³ doubled Poincaré | [1/cosh²(ρ/2), 1] |
| S³ gnomonic | [1, sec²ρ], only below π/2 |
| S³ doubled stereographic | [1, sec²(ρ/2)] |

A direct test with a box field never overestimated. Worst-case step efficiency at ρ = 1.2:

| Chart | Step efficiency |
|---|---|
| Klein | 0.39 |
| Poincaré | 0.79 |
| Gnomonic | 0.14 |

The cost is a declared chart and region radius, a shape that depends on the chart, and slower marching for larger objects. Recommendation: compile errors in the first subset, with this recorded as the cheapest later route (one generic wrapper).
- **Marching along the geodesic converges with near-Euclidean step counts** [checked-num]: Sphere tracing re-flowed from the ray's start each step, exact fields, tolerance 1e-4, 3000 rays. Mean steps:

| Case | E³ | H³ | S³ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ball, hits / misses | 10.3 / 16.6 | 10.2 / 16.1 | 11.6 / 17.9 |
| Torus, hits | 11.3 | 9.9 | 12.4 |

Flag [math]: march_epsilon(t) grows like 1 + t, a Euclidean pixel-footprint model (march.glsl:20). In H³ the footprint grows like sinh t, and in S³ like |sin t|, which shrinks near the antipode. The 5e-4 cap limits the effect. Also, the generated march uses ray.origin + t·ray.direction (index.ts:234), so it must go through the flow seam in curved spaces.
- **Finite-difference normals in the tangent frame work; the existing 4-tap stencil is first order** [checked-num]: Four taps along an orthonormal tangent frame at the hit point, against the analytic torus normal: 1.5e-3 rad error at step 1e-3, 1.5e-4 at 1e-4, and 1.6e-3 in float32 at 1e-3.

Plain linear taps q + h·k, which leave the manifold, are as accurate as taps along the geodesic. So no cosh/sinh per tap is needed, but a tangent frame at q is.

The error is first order even in E³. The tetrahedral stencil's third moments Σkₓk_yk_z do not vanish. The central 6-tap gives 2.2e-6 at the same step (tetra_e3.ts). The comment at src/components/geometry/index.ts:336 claiming "same order of accuracy" is false.

All the native primitives have closed-form normals, so finite differences are needed only for marched fields.

# light-sampling-twins

SUMMARY: Float64 Monte Carlo twins confirm that light sampling carries over to H3 and S3 when every Euclidean d² is replaced by A(r) = sinh²r (H3) or sin²r (S3), and cos at the light is taken against the geodesic's arrival velocity. Cosine/BSDF sampling (A), area sampling (B), the power-heuristic combination of A and B (MIS) and cone sampling (C) were compared across 75 irradiance cases at 4M samples each: disks and balls, on-axis, off-axis, tilted, crossing the horizon, far away, past π/2 in S3, and below the horizon in S3. All agree with each other and with the closed forms, with |z| ≤ 2.54. The three cases with |z| > 2.3 were rerun with new seeds and gave |z| ≤ 1.01. The cone half-angle sin α = S(R)/S(d) and the disk edge tan β = T(ρ)/S(D) are exact at a relative step of 1e-7. S3 has a second, backward cone, and a light below the horizon is seen through the long arc, which arrives from the emitter's other face. Point lights follow I·max(cos,0)/sinh²d in H3 and I·|cos|/sin²d in S3; for the matching ball, I = π·Le·S(R)². The equiangular pdf integrates to 1.000000000 in 9 cases, including S3 segments up to 2π, using an unwrapped atan2 form, and its estimates match Simpson. The closed furnace (6 bounces, ρ 0.8) passes for pt, pt-nee and pt-mis in H3 up to R=4 and in S3 up to R=3, including non-convex S3 balls with R > π/2. Main design consequences: (1) the current one-wi LightSample in S3 is unbiased if one arc is chosen at random, but measured up to ~67× more variance than summing both arcs; (2) H3 chained segments drift off the hyperboloid exponentially even in float64, and rebuilding each hit in the object's local frame fixes it; (3) area sampling in S3 has a near-light variance problem for lights near the antipode.

# camera-placement-authoring

SUMMARY: The simplest design keeps every authored type and every App parameter contract. An authored Vec3 point means exponential coordinates at the origin (exp_o(v)); in E³ that is v itself, so nothing Euclidean changes.

Camera: position and target stay reserved Vec3 parameters. In curved programs the four camera uniforms keep their names and become vec4 (P, F, R, U, an orthonormal tangent frame), computed by CPU closures. Pinhole and fisheye keep their formulas over vec4, except that normalize becomes a new ambient_normalize seam (Euclidean body: normalize).

Placement: a curved space has no fold and no scale. g = T(position)·R(rotation), uploaded as one mat4 Ginv (a baked literal when constant, a uniform when driven). Normals are recovered from Ginv alone with the J trick, and the inverse is exact (J gᵀ J or gᵀ).

Scene type: no new field. scene.ambientSpace already exists and is required. Fix CF-17 and settle the curvature field.

The one real design choice is the camera's up rule (holonomy). Origin-centered is simplest but makes an orbit around an off-origin target roll the horizon by up to 54° (H³) or 11° (S³). Target-centered makes an orbit an exact rotation about the target, but the roll depends on the look-ahead distance, and in H³ it needs a Gram–Schmidt cleanup.

A precision finding: a camera ray in float32 on the hyperboloid is wrong by 2e-4 at distance 4 from the model origin and by O(1) at distance 8. Local-frame intersection does not cure this; camera-relative coordinates would, at the cost of making every placement live.

# far-precision-media

SUMMARY: Answers to the four questions, labeled:

(1) Far limits. E3 stays at 1000. In S3, 2π is an exact search limit, and black is the exact answer for a ray that misses in vacuum. Cutting free flight at 2π inside an ambient medium is a large bias, though: e^{-2πσ} of the scattering is lost, which is 94% at σ=0.01. The exact fix is small: give the medium sampler an unbounded segment on a miss. The analytic body needs no change.

In H3, a declared clip of 10 in arc length is recommended. cosh overflows at t=89.42. Everything the clip cuts is sub-pixel: the band at a plane's ideal circle is about 2 sinh(h)·e^{-2T}, and the sky key does not depend on T. Ambient medium plus any sky should be a compile error in H3, because the sky would leak through with transmittance e^{-σT} when the exact value is 0.

The sky key is normalize((p+v).xyz), and it is checked. The direction at x toward an ideal point has a closed form. A table pdf over ideal points becomes a solid-angle pdf at x by dividing by ((x+v).w)²; this was checked by finite differences and quadrature. Simplest first version: a constant sky with NEE and MIS, sampled uniformly at x; image and procedural skies looked up by the key from BSDF rays only. Image-sky NEE and MIS need the Jacobian evaluated at the scattering vertex. After a null crossing, s.ray no longer holds that vertex.

(2) Epsilons. EPS_INTERFACE, MARCH_CLEARANCE and hit.eps are already arc lengths. SHADOW_BACKOFF is subtracted from a Euclidean length() and must use a geodesic distance. fp_uncertainty must take all four coordinates. Measured: H3 point error is about 2^-23·w if w is recomputed from xyz, but about 2^-23·w² with radial renormalization (thurston's method), which also fails outright at D≥8. Consequence: H3 scenes must stay within about D≈4.2 of the world origin, the analogue of |coord|<33 in E3. In S3, march_epsilon under-resolves near the antipode.

(3) Media. Beer-Lambert in arc length is unchanged. analytic.glsl:32 (exit_dir = ray.direction) is wrong off E3; it needs the flowed velocity. The same applies to ray_spawn at null crossings. Equiangular is entirely Euclidean; make it a compile error off E3.

(4) S3 two-arc NEE is required, or pt-nee will disagree with pt.

- **S3 far limit 2π is exact for geometry and vacuum** [checked-num + math]: Geodesics have period 2π. In float32, flowing a ray by TWO_PI returns it within 2.3e-7 of its start (s3.ts [1]). A ray that hits nothing on [0,2π) never hits anything. A camera ray that misses passes back through the geometry-free camera; along a closed source-free geodesic in vacuum the radiance is 0, so black is exact, not a truncation [math]. A ray leaving a closed surface always re-hits within one lap. A ray from an open quad or disk returns and hits its own back side at about 2π−eps, which is correct physics [math].
- **S3 ambient medium: cutting at 2π is a large bias; the exact version is cheap** [checked-num + checked-code]: P(free flight survives a lap) = e^{-2πσ}: 0.94 at σ=0.01, 0.53 at σ=0.1 (s3.ts [4]). Each lap retraces the same geodesic, so the exact value is the one-lap value/(1−e^{-2πσ}), 16× more at σ=0.01. Fix: on a miss, pass medium_sample a huge finite t_max instead of MAX_DIST (pt.ts:327,331). The event point cos t·p + sin t·v is exact for any t. medium_sample_analytic is unchanged, since σ=0 channels give exp(0)=1, not NaN (analytic.glsl:16,36). The float32 cost comes from the representation of t itself: 2e-6 at t≈100 (s3.ts [3]). GPU sin/cos accuracy at large arguments is unspecified [mem]; measure before adding mod(t, TWO_PI). This needs a second S3 constant (the miss segment length) next to the 2π search limit.
- **S3 needs two-arc NEE and an arc-aware shadow distance** [math + checked-code]: Two geodesics join any two points, of lengths d and 2π−d, both with falloff 1/sin²d. Short-arc-only NEE misses real direct light, so pt-nee ≠ pt. Every positional light sampler must pick an arc or sum both. The shadow walker re-aims with ambient_direction_to (media.glsl:59), which always picks the short arc. It needs 'distance along this ray to the target': d if <dir,u_short> > 0, else 2π−d. This is one small S3-only helper.
- **H3 far clip: declared T = 10 in arc length** [checked-num]: float32 cosh overflows at t=89.42 (h3_precision.ts [1]). The clip cuts a band of width ≈ 2 sinh(h)·e^{-2T} near a plane's ideal circle: 5e-9 rad at h=1, T=10 ([4], formula checked against the root tanh t = tanh h/cos θ). The shadow target light_p = flow(x,v,T) stays finite for T≤40. Its distance must use acosh(−<p,q>): the error is 2.5e-4 at D=4. The asinh form fails completely for far points (error = T at T≥20). The re-aim angle error is ≤1.6e-4 rad ([3]). So the H3 ambient_distance needs two branches, asinh for small d and acosh for large.
- **H3 ambient medium plus sky should be a compile error** [math]: Exact transmittance to the sky through an unbounded medium is 0. A clip at T leaks e^{-σT} (0.90 at σ=0.01, T=10). An unbounded segment puts event points beyond float32's range. With no sky the clip is effectively exact: events beyond T see only lights at distance ≥ T−R_scene, a relative weight of about 4e^{-2(T−R)}.
- **H3 sky key = normalize((p+v).xyz) in world coordinates** [checked-num]: p+v is null to 6.5e-14. Its Klein point is a unit vector, and Klein(γ(t)) converges to it (9e-7 rad at t=10). At the origin the key equals the view direction exactly (h3_sky.ts [1]). In float32 the key error is ≤2.3e-6 rad for ray origins at D≤2. At D=4 it is 1.6e-5 for random rays and 1.2e-4 for rays aimed back toward the origin; at D=6 it is 3.5e-3 ([5]). A 4k equirect texel is ≈1.5e-3 rad. Today the miss lookup uses s.ray.direction (kernel.glsl:13), so a seam vec3 ambient_sky_key(p,v) is needed; the E3 body is `return v;`.
- **H3 env importance sampling: closed-form direction and Jacobian** [checked-num]: Direction at x toward ideal point ξ, with ξ̂=(ξ,1): v = (ξ̂ + <x,ξ̂>x)/(−<x,ξ̂>). It is unit and tangent, and key(x,v)=ξ to 1e-13 ([2]). dΩ_origin/dΩ_x = 1/((x+v).w)² = <x,ξ̂>² (finite differences 9e-5; quadrature ∫J dΩ_x/4π = 1.000000 for D=0..4; [3],[6]). So pdf_x(v) = p_table(key)/((x+v).w)². The NEE weight spread is [e^{-2D}, e^{2D}]. Trap [math]: x(t)+v(t) = e^t(x+v), so the Jacobian changes along the ray. The MIS pdf must be evaluated at the scattering vertex, but s.ray is re-spawned at null crossings (pt.ts:410). Also, environment_pdf takes only a direction (combiner.ts:60-61).
- **Simplest first H3 sky** [math]: Constant sky: NEE and MIS by uniform sampling at x in a tangent basis. The pdf 1/4π does not depend on x, so null crossings are harmless and no key is needed. Image and procedural skies: lookup by key from BSDF rays only (environment not samplable in H3; this is an estimator choice, so the converged image is unchanged). Later: NEE and MIS through the origin table with the Jacobian, plus one PathState float for the vertex. Directional lights: E3 only.
- **fp_uncertainty must read all four coordinates, and H3 renormalization must recompute w** [checked-num + checked-code]: math.glsl:54-56 reads only x,y,z. Measured H3 arc error at distance D from the origin (h3_precision.ts [2b]): radial p/√(−<p,p>) gives ≈2^-23·w², which is 2.5e-4 at D=4 and 1.2e-2 at D=6, and it fails to renormalize at D≥8 (993/2000 cases fail at D=12). This is thurston's hypNormalize, thurston-rebuild hyp/geometry/Point.js:15-20. Recomputing w=√(1+|xyz|²) gives ≈2^-23·w: 3.1e-6 at D=4, 1.6e-4 at D=8. Rule: recompute w; fp_uncertainty = max(ABS, REL·max over all four |p_i|) = REL·w. Measured error/fp_uncertainty stays ≤0.6. In S3 the error is ≈1e-7 everywhere and radial renormalization is fine (s3.ts [2]).
- **H3 working radius ≈ 4.2 from the world origin** [checked-num + checked-code]: REL·w exceeds EPS_INTERFACE = MARCH_CLEARANCE = 1e-3 at w>33, i.e. D>4.2. That is the exact analogue of |coord|>33 in E3, where the §4.2 classification band breaks (geometry/index.ts:218; E3 warning at Validator.ts:1684). - **Other epsilons: arc lengths already, except SHADOW_BACKOFF and S3 march_epsilon** [checked-code + math]: EPS_INTERFACE (intersection.ts:1693,1701), hit.eps (ray.glsl:31) and MARCH_CLEARANCE (geometry/index.ts:296) are passed as arc lengths, so no change. SHADOW_BACKOFF is subtracted from Euclidean length() (opaque.glsl:17, media.glsl:39), so the walkers need ambient_distance. march_epsilon = min(5e-4, 1e-4(1+t)) (march.glsl:20) is conservative in H3, since the footprint grows like sinh t ≥ t. - **Homogeneous media: Beer-Lambert is fine; exit_dir and ray_spawn directions are wrong off E3** [checked-code]: analytic.glsl:9-43 and emission.glsl use only arc length t and ambient_geodesic, so they are correct given arc-length hits. analytic.glsl:32 `ms.exit_dir = ray.direction` must become the flowed velocity (H3: sinh t·p + cosh t·v; S3: −sin t·p + cos t·v). The same issue appears at null crossings: ray_spawn(hit, s.ray.direction) (pt.ts:410, media.glsl:58) dots an origin-space tangent with hit.ng at hit.p (ray.glsl:30). Phase functions use raw dot + build_basis(vec3) (hg.glsl:13,30; rayleigh.glsl:11,25). The R⁴ dot is the metric in S3 but not in H3, and there is no vec4 basis, so phase sampling needs a tangent basis at the event point.
