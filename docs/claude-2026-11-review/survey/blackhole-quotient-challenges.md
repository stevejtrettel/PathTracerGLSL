# Black-hole and quotient research: skeptic reviews

Each feasibility report (bh-code.md, bh-literature.md, quotients.md in this folder) was attacked by a skeptic. Their corrected verdicts and corrections follow; where they conflict with the report, the skeptic's version is the later and more careful one, but it is still reasoning unless marked as read or measured.

## charged-blackholes-code

**Report verdict.** Exact next-event estimation (two-point connections) for multi-hole Majumdar–Papapetrou in a WebGL2 fragment shader is not feasible reliably: note it and skip it. Reasons:
- Images cannot be labeled in 3D.
- The dynamics are chaotic, with infinitely many images.
- Newton shooting fails on 1.5–11.5% of pairs even in float64 (measured).
- Each solve costs 5–16 full marches.
- Seed-dependent failures make the light-sampling pdf unknowable from the BSDF side, so the MIS weights would carry a bias.

For a spherically symmetric hole (Schwarzschild, or one Majumdar–Papapetrou hole), exact next-event estimation is plausible but unchecked. It needs:
- a 1D root find for the impact parameter b per image index k;
- labeling of BSDF rays by swept azimuth;
- a closed-form Jacobian;
- k ≤ 1–2 in float32.

Even then, the shadow ray is a full curved march.

Recommended path:
- Keep the built BSDF-only "glass rule": unbiased, already working for Majumdar–Papapetrou.
- If light sampling is wanted, add forward aimed direction sampling for area lights. Its pdf is known in closed form at the shading point, so it needs no connection, Jacobian or image list, and it stays unbiased whatever the guess. The owner already built this in PathTracer.
- Declare delta lights (point, spot, directional) unsupported inside curved regions, unless spherical symmetry makes an exact solve worthwhile.
- The CPU float64 tools in charged-blackholes are the right reference for validating GLSL marches, not something to port.

**Corrected verdict.** The headline verdict stands with changes. (1) Exact two-point NEE for multi-hole Majumdar–Papapetrou in WebGL2: not feasible reliably. Note it and skip it. But the reason is weaker than stated. The obstacle is not the missing image label, since any computable property of the path can define the class set K. The obstacles are that no solve can be shown complete within K and that a Jacobian that is only approximately right makes the estimator biased. The cost alone also rules it out: about 5–16 marches per vertex, each step costing about 7 field evaluations and a region lookup. (2) Schwarzschild and a single MP hole (extremal RN): feasible, and likely cheaper than what PTG does now. This holds only in PTG's current setting: the hole is a spherically symmetric lens region with nothing inside it (the 'geometry-free interior', grin.md, read). There the whole traversal is a function of one number, the impact parameter b. By Bouguer's invariant n·r·sinθ, the exit map and capture (b < b_c) come from a 1D deflection table δ(b). It is computed once in float64 on the CPU and tabulated in log(b−b_c). BSDF rays and NEE then use the same map, so MIS is exactly consistent. Two-point NEE becomes a bracketed 1D root find per image, with a closed-form Jacobian. The only bias is the table's interpolation error, which must be declared. Status: thought through, not checked. In particular, monotonicity of δ(b) on each branch for a truncated ball with a dielectric wall is unverified. A 'true spacetime' scene, where the field is global and objects sit inside it, is a different and much harder project. Every ray is curved there, and curved-ray intersection with scene geometry is unbuilt in PTG (deferred). Its cost is steps × scene query per ray. It has not been measured and should not be promised. (3) Aimed forward sampling, a mixture of the BSDF lobe and a lobe aimed at the light, is unbiased with any guess, provided the BSDF component covers the support and both the mixture and its pdf are evaluated exactly. Its variance benefit in curved space has not been measured. (4) Delta lights: with the existing glass rule, illumination from delta lights through the bent region is already lost under the declared truncation shadows: 'opaque-dielectrics' (read in source). Exact NEE for images k ≤ K would recover part of it. Images beyond K remain a declared bias for delta lights, because BSDF sampling cannot pick them up. (5) PTG today renders an analog of MP: a dielectric block or ball whose interior has the MP index. The field is truncated at a Fresnel wall. This is not the spacetime. Its image is unbiased for that analog with area lights and the environment, up to the declared truncations. (6) The uniqueness assumption in ambient_direction_to fails already for S³ (antipodal caustic, short and long arcs, windings) and for S²×R, which are on the planned list. It does not fail for E³, H³ or H²×R. So the seam needs a multi-image design before any black-hole or quotient work.

**Corrections**

- §0.3 / §5: 'PathTracerGLSL already renders MP black holes … the image is unbiased' overstates. Read in source: demos/blackholeScene.ts:13-20 and accretionScene.ts:9-11 put the MP field inside a dielectric box or ball. The field is truncated at a wall that refracts with the local n, about 1.25 at the ball (Fresnel reflection, no field outside). Both demos use directLighting: 'none' (blackholeScene.ts:76, accretionScene.ts:67). So the scene is a transformation-optics analog, not the spacetime. 'Unbiased' holds only relative to that analog, for area lights and the environment, and only up to declared truncations: Verlet truncation error, capture at n>50, the GRIN_MAX_ROUNDS hard stop (grin.glsl:36-58), and the loss of all bent delta-light illumination under shadows: 'opaque-dielectrics' (materials.ts:829-835, shadow/media/media.md:19).
- §0.3 glass-rule claim: verified, read in source. medium_transmittance returns SPECTRUM_ZERO for deflecting media (src/compiler/generate/features/materials.ts:829-835). A traversal is recorded as a delta event with kernel_record(s, 1.0, …) (src/components/transport/integrators/pt/pt.ts:342-358), so emitters reached by bent rays score full weight. For area lights this is unbiased, because NEE's pdf for a bent path is zero and the BSDF weight is 1. It does NOT hold for delta lights: their bent contribution is dropped, and only the opaque-dielectrics ledger entry declares that.
- §4 Jacobian formula is wrong. Identically, |sinΔφ| = |sin(ψ+2πk)| = |sin(2π(k+1)−ψ)| = sinψ, so the stated out-of-plane factor 'sinΔφ / sinψ' is ±1 and carries no information. Reasoning, not checked: with emission angle α measured from the radial direction at x (static-observer frame) and azimuth β about it, dΩ_x = sinα dα dβ. The image on the sphere of radius r_y has dA_sphere = r_y² sinψ |dΔφ/dα| dα dβ. The beam cross-section adds |cos α_y|, the arrival angle to the radial direction. Projection onto the light adds 1/|cosθ_light|. So dA_light/dΩ_x = r_y² sinψ |dΔφ/dα| |cos α_y| / (sinα |cosθ_light|). The factor is sinψ/sinα, not sinΔφ/sinψ. It is singular at ψ ∈ {0, π}, the Einstein-ring caustic, and that must be handled. It must also be written consistently in the renderer's coordinate measure (isotropic coordinates with the L/n² convention), not in areal radius.
- §4 'remaining images … left to BSDF sampling with MIS weight 1' is right for area lights and wrong for delta lights. BSDF sampling cannot hit a point, spot or directional light, so every image outside K is lost: a bias to declare, not variance. The same applies to quotients (§6). There the share outside K does not shrink with the radius in E³ or H³: the image count grows like the shell area while each image's solid angle falls like 1/area, so every shell contributes comparably until occlusion cuts it off. Reasoning.
- §4 mechanism of the bias from 'random solver failure' is misstated. The MIS weights still sum to 1, because both sides evaluate the same formula. The bias comes from NEE dividing by p_A·J while the actual density is p_A·J·P(success). The conclusion (bias = −∫ f·w_nee·(1−P_success)) is right. The report also misses that this is not inevitable in principle: Specular Manifold Sampling (Zeltner, Georgiev, Jakob, ACM TOG 39(4), SIGGRAPH 2020; from memory) estimates 1/P(success) without bias by repeating Bernoulli trials of a randomly seeded Newton solver. It does so only for a technique used without MIS. Its expected cost is 1/P per sample, unbounded for images with tiny basins, so it is infeasible in a WebGL2 fragment shader. It should be cited as the known unbiased way to use a solver that can fail. Manifold NEE (Hanika, Droske, Fascione, EGSR 2015; from memory) is the biased predecessor.
- §4 for multi-hole MP, condition (b) 'no class label in 3D' is not the obstacle. K may be defined by any property of the path that the BSDF side can compute from its own ray: total turning angle, the sequence of near-approaches to the holes (Shipley–Dolan's symbolic dynamics, basin-maps.md:10-30, read), or minimum distance to each hole. The real obstacle is (c), a solve that is complete within K, plus the cost. Rewrite the reasoning; the verdict does not change.
- §2.1 measurement over-reads its data. Read shoot.ts. shootOnce returns null when the flat-direction ray is captured or escapes before its first closest approach to y. solve() then fails at iteration 0 without trying another seed. The residual 'first closest approach' is also discontinuous in the direction. So 1.5–11.5% is the failure rate of this unglobalized Gauss–Newton, not a property of the problem. Continuation in M, multi-start or a better residual could lower it. Separately, the test geometry (holes at ±1, so a = 2, with M up to 2) enters Shipley–Dolan's band of stable bounded null orbits (0.770M < a < 1.089M, basin-maps.md:26-31, read) at M = 2. That should be stated when interpreting the results.
- §3 'A finite-difference Jacobian is impossible at float32 (h = 1e-6 below the noise)' overstates. With h ≈ √eps·scale ≈ 3e-4, FD gives about 3–4 correct digits. That is enough for Newton, which only needs an approximate Jacobian; the final residual check decides acceptance. The real point is different: the Jacobian that enters the ESTIMATOR (dA/dω in the NEE weight and the MIS pdf) must be exact or declared. An FD or float32 Jacobian there is a bias of the same relative size as its error. Reasoning.
- §3 DOP853 row: the quote is verified (CB/docs/plans/upgrade.md:241-245, read). But the row argues against the wrong thing. DOP853 at 1e-10 is pointless in float32, yet an embedded adaptive RK (DOPRI5, 7 stages × 6 floats) at float32 tolerances is feasible in GLSL. The owner's own plan prefers a 4th-order splitting (Forest–Ruth/SABA) on the Maupertuis form, whose force depends on position only. PTG's walker already uses that form at 2nd order (Verlet, grin.glsl:8-12, read). Also, §1.2 says '16 stages' and §3 says '13 stages': pick one.
- §3 GPU cost is underestimated. The '~180 steps per black-hole ray' figure belongs to PT's controller marcher (PT/docs/curved-light-blackhole.md:40-66, read). The same doc reports ~1750 steps with a fixed 0.03 cap. PTG's walker has no force-change controller. It uses h = min(GRIN_STEP = 0.02, DS_MAX/n, DTOL/|∇n|) (grin.glsl:59-60, 231, read), so a weak field gives about 50 steps per unit length. Each step evaluates ior_at 7 times (central-difference gradient, grin.glsl:108-114), plus scene_region_at + material_of (grin_inside, :127-129, :249) and scene_medium_properties (:272), with up to 102,400 steps (:215). So the 'U and ∇U together, O(N), ~15 flops per hole' kernel (CB) is not what PTG pays: PTG pays about 7× the field cost plus a region query per step. Every NEE design based on marching (exact NEE = ≥1 extra march per image per vertex; Newton = 5–16 marches) multiplies this.
- §4 'The shadow ray is still a full curved march … through the scene's SDFs': PTG has no curved-ray intersection with embedded geometry at all. grin.md (read) says 'Geometry-free interior (a bent ray hitting embedded surfaces is the deferred curved-ray-intersection problem)'. Any Schwarzschild plan that puts objects inside the field depends on a large unbuilt feature. The report should split two cases. (i) A lens region with an empty interior: tractable. (ii) A global spacetime with objects in it: unbuilt, cost unmeasured.
- Missed approach, and the most important one (thought through, not checked). For a spherically symmetric hole in an object-free ball, Bouguer's invariant n·r·sinθ (which also survives Snell refraction at a spherical wall) makes the ball's whole effect a function of b. The ingredients are capture for b < b_c, the deflection δ(b) = π − 2∫ b dr / (r √(n²r² − b²)), and the exit point by symmetry. Tabulate δ(b) on the CPU in float64, in the variable log(b − b_c), as a 1D texture. Then: a BSDF traversal costs O(1) instead of hundreds of steps. NEE through the ball for primary and higher images is a bracketed (not Newton) 1D root find in the plane of x, y and the center. The Jacobian comes from dδ/db. And because BSDF rays and NEE share one exit map, the 'analytic-versus-marched mismatch' bias of §4 disappears; only the declared table error remains. Environment NEE (a point-to-direction problem) is the same 1D solve. Caveats: Fresnel at a dielectric wall turns internal reflections into extra image families. Truncating Schwarzschild at radius R leaves an index jump n(R) ≈ 1 + 2M/R and drops the weak-field deflection outside R; both must be declared. The emission integral of an accretion disk breaks the symmetry, so emission along the path would still need marching or a 2D table.
- Missed approach: for Schwarzschild the ray itself has a closed form, u(φ) in Weierstrass ℘ (or Jacobi elliptic) functions from the Binet equation u'' = −u + 3Mu² (from memory). For a global-field scene this gives exact positions along the ray, with intersection by chords whose error is bounded by the sagitta, instead of accumulating Verlet error. For extremal RN, (du/dφ)² = 1/b² − u²(1−Mu)² factors into two quadratics, so it is also elliptic. Reasoning.
- §4 float32 image limit: the binding constraint for a marched BSDF ray is the walker's truncation error, amplified by e^{2π} ≈ 535 per orbit (Schwarzschild) or e^{√2π} ≈ 85 (extremal RN), not float eps. Under the tabulated approach the limit is float32 resolution of b − b_c. Parameterize by δb = b − b_c to avoid cancellation. That allows about k ≤ 2 for Schwarzschild (e^{−4π} ≈ 3.5e-6) and about k ≤ 3 for extremal RN (e^{−3√2π} ≈ 1.6e-6). The report's 'K = 1, perhaps 2' undercounts RN. The multipliers themselves check out by my own derivation: λ = 1/(4√2 m), and with period 8πm that gives e^{√2π}.
- §4 open question 3 (monotonicity of Δφ(b)): b is the wrong parameter. For a given b there are two rays from x, ingoing and outgoing, and rays starting inside the photon sphere have apoapses. Parameterize by the emission angle α at x and handle the before- and after-periapsis branches. Perlick's exact lens equation for static spherically symmetric spacetimes (Phys. Rev. D 69, 064017, 2004; from memory) treats observer and source at finite radii and should be read before claiming monotonicity. Bozza's strong-deflection limit (2002) is the source for the e^{2π} image ratio, which holds asymptotically.
- §5 radiometry: MP agrees between GR and the glass analog only in BOLOMETRIC radiance (U⁴ = n²). Verified by my own derivation, as is the Schwarzschild mismatch AB = 1 − M²/4r². Per wavelength they disagree even for MP: GR shifts λ by the ratio of U at emitter and observer, while the analog keeps λ fixed. So the color of an RGB or spectral render is wrong for MP too, not only for Schwarzschild. Also from reasoning: the analog evaluates Fresnel at embedded dielectric interfaces against the local n(x), while in GR the local vacuum index is 1. This matters only once embedded geometry exists.
- §5 'Black holes (and quotients) break uniqueness' of ambient_direction_to (README.md:13, read): incomplete. S³ (short arc, long arc, windings, and a whole 2-sphere of geodesics at the antipode) and S²×R also break it. Both are on the owner's planned list. E³, H³ and H²×R (simply connected, nonpositive curvature) do not. So the multi-image seam is needed for the analytic spaces too, and it should be designed once for S³, quotients and lens regions.
- §4 aimed forward sampling: unbiased provided the guess is a deterministic function of x, so that p_mix(ω) can be evaluated for any ω, and the BSDF component has positive weight wherever f > 0. When standard NEE is kept for lights reached by straight segments, p_mix (not p_bsdf) must go into the MIS weights. A 3D direction table per light costs texture memory per light. Cells reached by several images or by none (shadow) need a rule, and a coarse table only costs variance. The report should say that this is a primitive form of path guiding (Müller, Gross, Novák, 'Practical Path Guiding', EGSR 2017; from memory). Online learning of a guide in WebGL2 would need scattered additive writes to textures: possible but awkward.
- §6 quotients: 'much more tractable' is right in structure, but add two points. NEE cost scales with |K| shadow rays per vertex unless one image is sampled from K with known probabilities, which is unbiased and costs a constant per vertex. And BSDF rays in a nearly empty quotient wander until MAX_DIST (1000), the existing declared far clip.

**Missed approaches**

- A spherically symmetric lens region as an O(1) primitive: a CPU float64 deflection table δ(b), tabulated in log(b − b_c), shared by BSDF rays and NEE. Two-point NEE becomes a bracketed 1D root find per image with a closed-form Jacobian (thought through, not checked)
- Closed-form Schwarzschild and extremal-RN orbits (Weierstrass ℘ / elliptic functions from the Binet equation) in place of marching (from memory)
- Specular Manifold Sampling (Zeltner, Georgiev, Jakob 2020): unbiased reciprocal-probability estimation for a randomly seeded solver that can fail. It refutes 'solver failure ⇒ necessarily biased', but its cost is unbounded and infeasible in WebGL2 (from memory)
- Manifold NEE (Hanika et al. 2015) as the biased reference point (from memory)
- Perlick's exact lens equation (PRD 69, 064017, 2004) and Bozza's strong-deflection limit as the sources for image enumeration and magnification at finite radii (from memory)
- Path guiding (Müller et al. 2017) as the general form of aimed forward sampling with a precomputed or learned guide (from memory)
- Sampling one image from K with known probabilities, instead of all |K| shadow rays (reasoning)
- Ray-bundle (Jacobi-field) footprints as in DNGR (James et al., CQG 32 065001, 2015) for the exact NEE Jacobian in non-symmetric fields (from memory)


## blackhole-nee-literature

**Report verdict.** Emission-only and big-emitter black-hole scenes in both Schwarzschild and MP work in WebGL2 now, using BSDF sampling only and adding redshift factors. That is what the existing MP demo does.

Schwarzschild NEE with exact connections is plausible in WebGL2:
- The connection is a planar 1D root find with Perlick's closed-form Jacobian.
- NEE handles the two lowest-order images; all other images go to BSDF sampling at weight 1.
- It is unbiased by my reasoning, costs roughly 2-3× per bounce, and is bounded and fixed-iteration.
- It has never been built or tested anywhere I could find. It deserves its own research plan, starting with a TypeScript twin.

MP NEE with exact connections: not feasible reliably or efficiently in WebGL2. Note it and skip it.
- The dynamics are chaotic, so connections come in infinite fractal families.
- The only unbiased scheme (Booth/SMS reciprocal) has an unbounded loop, huge variance and no MIS pdf.
- Float32 cannot resolve high-order connections, and WebGPU would not fix precision either.
- For MP: use BSDF sampling only, or the glass rule with a declared, compactly supported field.

Both spacetimes share an earlier blocker that matters more than NEE: scattering surfaces inside the curved region need per-step scene intersection. The GRIN walker's interior is geometry-free today. This is the dominant cost and must be designed first.

**Corrected verdict.** I partly agree with the report. Its physics and its main bias argument hold. Its MP verdict is too pessimistic for area lights and the sky, and too optimistic for delta lights. It also missed the simplest unbiased option.

(1) Pictures with emission only, and pt with BSDF sampling only, are feasible now in both spacetimes. I agree with the report here.

(2) Light sampling for AREA lights and the ENVIRONMENT can be made unbiased in BOTH Schwarzschild and MP without any exact two-point solve [reasoning, the standard Veach one-sample MIS argument; not built or tested].
- How it works: sample a DIRECTION at x from a distribution q(w) that is a deterministic function of (x, light). An example is a uniform cone around a predicted image direction, where the prediction comes from any approximate solver with a fixed iteration count. Trace the same numerical geodesic a BSDF ray would follow, and score whatever it actually hits with f*L*cos/q. Combine this with BSDF sampling by MIS in solid angle. At BSDF emitter hits, q is evaluated by recomputing the same deterministic guide.
- Why it is unbiased: the pdf is exact by construction. Solver error, float32 precision, chaotic basins and the missing bundle Jacobian (D_ang, Jacobi fields) affect only variance, never bias.
- Cost: one curved trace per NEE sample (the same as a BSDF ray), plus a cheap guide, plus one guide recomputation per BSDF emitter hit. All loops are fixed-length, so this fits WebGL2.
- For Schwarzschild the guide can be the planar root find, with the cone sized from Perlick's D_r and D_t. For MP it can be a few Newton steps from a single-hole seed. Efficiency in chaotic regions is unknown and has to be measured.
- The tracer's existing sphere-light cone sampling and environment importance sampling are already direction-space techniques. They become unbiased in curved space once the shadow ray is traced as a geodesic and scores what it hits, instead of assuming it reaches a precomputed point.

(3) Exact NEE in the area measure for Schwarzschild (Perlick Jacobian plus the MIS partition) is plausible but not needed. It is more complex than (2) and needs the analytic orbit (the Weierstrass closed form, not the Binet ODE) for consistency. Its only advantage is lower variance for very small lights. Treat it as an optional later study.

(4) DELTA lights (point, spot, beam, the directional light, and the delta-light-only equiangular medium NEE) are the real hard case, and the report's partition argument does not cover them. BSDF sampling can never reach a delta light, so every image NEE omits is lost, which is bias.
- Schwarzschild: exact connection to images k <= K, with the higher images declared as a truncation (each further winding carries roughly e^-2pi, about 1/535, of the flux). Feasible but unbuilt.
- MP: not feasible in WebGL2. Disallow delta lights inside the field, or keep them outside it behind the existing opaque-shadow glass rule, which is a declared truncation.

(5) The geometry-free GRIN interior is the first blocker, as the report says. Per-step scene intersection is the dominant cost and has not been measured. In WebGL2 it also brings shader size and compile time (keep one scene_intersect call site inside one loop over trace requests), worst-lane divergence, and GPU-watchdog risk from long trapped traversals (the hard stop is 102,400 steps).

Summary:
- Area lights and the sky: feasible and unbiased in both spacetimes via (2).
- Delta lights: Schwarzschild needs a declared image truncation; MP: note and skip.
- Exact area-measure NEE: optional research.
- Kerr: out of scope, as the report says.

## quotient-light

**Report verdict.** Feasible in WebGL2, with conditions. Compact quotients of E³, S³, H³ and H²×R can be rendered correctly and efficiently if four things hold: lights are area or volume emitters; NEE samples a fixed, computable set of images (with both arcs in S³) and BSDF sampling covers the other images under partition MIS; rays cross cells by exact closed-form face exits and every face-crossing object is included as a ghost copy; and the far clip per segment is a declared measurement field per space, exact at 2π in S³. Nothing needs compute shaders or WebGPU; WebGPU would only schedule the slowest rays better. Note and skip for now: delta lights in quotients (no BSDF fallback, and their image sums may diverge or cost exponentially), the environment and directional lights in compact quotients, cusped manifolds without a truncating surface, and non-orientable quotients. Everything except E³ quotients waits on the curved ambient spaces. Proposed first milestone: the cubical 3-torus. Objects stay strictly inside D. Lights are emitters only. NEE uses the in-cell image plus the 6 face neighbors with partition MIS. The teleport loop goes inside scene_intersect, with an ivec3 cell offset on Hit; this changes a locked type and needs the owner's approval. Witnesses: pt = pt-nee = pt-mis; an empty torus filled with an emitting, absorbing medium, whose radiance is exactly (Le/σa)(1−e^{−σa R}); a closed furnace with all corridors blocked, L = Σ_{n≤N} ρⁿ·Le; and zero teleport-cap hits.

**Corrected verdict.** Corrected verdict: plausibly feasible in WebGL2, but nothing has been measured, and several claims are wrong as written. Everything in the report is [reasoned] apart from the cited code, and I checked the code citations I sampled. Nothing in it is numerically confirmed. What holds: the partition-MIS rule is ordinary MIS bookkeeping (Veach's condition is that weights sum to 1 over the techniques with nonzero pdf, and a technique with pdf 0 gets weight 0). That is the same mechanism pbrt uses for lights that only BSDF sampling can reach [memory]. So unbiasedness for area and volume emitters under a declared per-segment far clip is correct, on two conditions. First, the BSDF-side identification of the image must be exactly the same function as the NEE-side choice. Second, that identification must be defined on light-image group elements, not on cell elements or words. The E³/Z³ first milestone is sound and needs no curved-space work. It does need the owner to approve a change to the locked Hit/trace-loop types. What does not hold: (1) The S³ two-arc treatment double-counts or misdescribes geodesics whenever −I ∈ Γ. That covers the two spherical groups thurston-rebuild ships (quaternion, poincare) [their presence in thurston-rebuild read; −I ∈ Γ from memory]. (2) "A far clip of 2π is exact in S³/Γ" is false once media are present. (3) "Teleporting inside scene_intersect changes only NEE selection and MIS identification" is false for heterogeneous media and the tracking walk. (4) The blanket ban on delta lights is too broad: spherical quotients have finitely many images, so point lights are exact there. (5) The "exponential escape" claim for H²×R quotients is wrong, because vertical corridors give heavy tails. (6) Two of the four proposed witnesses cannot detect a wrong face pairing. (7) The claim that the unrolled crystal "cannot be an exact witness" is wrong for finite-horizon E³ scenes. Cost is underestimated in two places: the uniform budget (S³ group tables, and a program that already uses uniforms), and GPU tail latency and watchdog limits from nested loops. Revised recommendation: keep E³/Z³ as the first milestone. Prove partition MIS with a TypeScript twin and a pt = pt-nee = pt-mis witness. Add an exact crystal-unrolling cross-check witness. Defer S³ quotients until the arc bookkeeping is redone over distinct geodesics of M (the elliptic-space reduction when −I ∈ Γ). Treat H²×R like E³ (heavy tails), not like H³. Keep the black-hole cross-link (§2.5) explicitly unproven. It is unbiased only if the NEE pdf is the exact density of the geodesic actually traced, and an approximate two-point solve does not give that.

**Corrections**

- S³ arcs when −I ∈ Γ (§2.1, §2.2c, §5) [reasoned; thurston-rebuild sph/groups lists 'quaternion' and 'poincare' (read); that Q8 and the binary icosahedral group contain −I is from memory]: on the great circle through x̃ and z = γỹ, the points come in the order x̃ (0), z (d), −x̃ (π), −z (π+d). The major arc to z leaves x̃ along −ω, the same initial direction as the minor arc to −z, and passes through −z = (−γ)ỹ first, at distance π−d. So when −I ∈ Γ (Q8, binary tetrahedral, binary icosahedral = Poincaré dodecahedral space, L(p,q) with p even), the 'major arc of γ' is the continuation of the 'minor arc of −γ' through that image. If emitters occlude shadow rays, it is always blocked; if they don't, counting both double-counts. S(x) must range over distinct geodesic segments of M. When −I ∈ Γ, work in elliptic space RP³ = S³/±1, where geodesics have length π and there is one arc per element of Γ/±1. thurston-rebuild's sph localPointLight returns dir and negate(dir) unconditionally (geometries/sph/lights/localPointLight/shaders/struct.glsl:28-45, read), so it has the same issue.
- S³ 'half the direct light goes to the major arc' (§2.2c) is wrong at opaque surfaces [reasoned]: the minor-arc direction ω and the major-arc direction −ω lie in opposite hemispheres. At a reflecting surface, at most one arc per light point has nonzero BSDF; the major arc carries light from points 'below the horizon'. Both arcs matter at medium vertices and on transmissive surfaces. Keeping both in S(x) is still correct; the variance argument for it is not.
- 'Far clip 2π in S³/Γ is exact' (§3.1, §3.4, §5, §7) holds only in vacuum [reasoned]. With a medium, a geodesic that wraps keeps accumulating: radiance along a closed great circle is L_{[0,2π]}/(1 − T(2π)), and clipping at 2π drops the fraction T(2π) = e^{−σ_t·2π}. The same goes for medium vertices: from x in a medium, geodesics of length d + 2πk pass back through x and are not blocked. Clip 2π is exact only for rays from surface points through vacuum; otherwise it is a declared truncation.
- Option (i), 'teleport inside scene_intersect; transport changes only in NEE image selection and MIS identification' (§4.5) is false once media are present [reasoned]: delta tracking and shadow_transmittance evaluate density at points along a segment that spans several cells. In H³ those points must be reduced cell by cell; positions continued past D in X lose precision exponentially, the very thing teleporting is meant to avoid. In E³ a Γ-periodic field can be reduced by floor(), so E³ survives. In H³, S³ and H²×R, the tracking walk must see the cell boundaries, so (i) does not hide teleports from transport in media scenes. Null-interface and innermost-wins state must also persist across teleports while inside a ghost copy.
- Image identification by word or teleport count is wrong outside the cube torus (§2.2c 'Shortcut: if more than (max word length) teleports since the vertex, γ ∉ S'; §4.4 'store a word or index of at most 2 letters') [reasoned]: in a general Dirichlet domain, D ∪ gD is not convex. A geodesic from x ∈ D to a point of gD can pass through a third cell near an edge, so it makes more teleports than |g|. Distinct words also represent the same element through edge-cycle relations (length 5 for Seifert–Weber). Membership must compare group elements: an ivec3 in E³ lattices, matrices with tolerance in H³ and S³. The shortcut is valid only where D ∪ gD is convex for every g in S, e.g. the cube torus with S = face neighbors.
- Identification must be per light image, not per cell, once ghost copies exist (§2.3, §4.1) [reasoned]: a hit on ghost copy g'·L inside cell γD belongs to light image γg'·L. S(x) and p_sel must be keyed on γg' (and the arc). The cell's accumulated cellBoost alone is not enough.
- Delta-light ban is too broad (§0, §3.3) [reasoned]: in S³/Γ, Γ is finite and geodesics of length < 2π are finite in number, so a point light has finitely many images (|Γ|, or |Γ/±1| in RP³), and summing or sampling them is exact with no truncation. The ban is justified only for infinite Γ (E³, H³, H²×R). Note also the antipodal caustic: irradiance at distance r is I/sin² r, which diverges at r → π. It is integrable on a surface only in the same marginal way as the 1/r² singularity at the light itself, so expect fireflies.
- H²×R is not 'like H³ horizontally' (§3.1, §4.2) [reasoned]: the geodesic flow of Σ×S¹ is a product and is not Anosov; it has a zero exponent along R. Geodesics near vertical move slowly in Σ and wind around S¹, so if some vertical circle avoids every obstacle, free paths have polynomial (corridor-like) tails, as in E³ tori. The escape-rate and shadowing arguments do not transfer.
- H²×R geometric term (§2.1, §2.3, §3.1) [reasoned]: the space is anisotropic. The exponential-map Jacobian depends on the elevation φ, about s·sinh(s cos φ)/cos φ (it reduces to s² in the flat case). It is not one function J(r), so the shell-counting formulas and the 'd² becomes sinh² or sin²' seam need a direction-dependent G.
- Truncation error bound (§3.1) [reasoned]: a clipped segment loses all radiance beyond R, not only direct emission. The bound is L_max × (fraction of directions clipped), with L_max ≤ Le_max/(1 − ρ_max) when that is finite, and it compounds per bounce. 'max Le × fraction' understates it, badly in closed scenes with ρ near 1.
- 'Three orthogonal slabs give about 1/t³' (§3.1, §8 witness 3) is wrong [reasoned]: with full planes x=c, y=c, z=c in the torus, a ray avoiding all three would need |d_x|, |d_y|, |d_z| all < L/t, which is impossible for unit d once t > L√3. Free flight is then bounded (finite horizon, zero tail), and the scene is optically a box with at most about 3 teleports per segment. That is fine for a leak test, but it says nothing about long-path behavior.
- Closed-furnace witness (§3.2, §8.3) cannot detect wrong face pairings [reasoned]: with uniform Le and ρ everywhere, any ray that hits any surface returns the same value, so a wrong pairing matrix, wrong orientation or wrong cell still passes. It detects only leaks, gaps and cap hits. Witness 2 (uniform medium) has the same blindness: a scene with no quotient at all also passes it.
- Witness 2's formula uses the wrong emission convention for this project [read: docs/fable-volumetric-component.md §3, 'Le·σ_a/σ_t·(1−e^{−σ_t·t})']: the source term is σ_a·Le, so a purely absorbing emitting medium gives Le·(1 − e^{−σ_a R}), not (Le/σ_a)(1 − e^{−σ_a R}).
- 'Unrolling into a crystal cannot serve as an exact witness' (§5, §7 B') is wrong for finite-horizon E³ scenes [reasoned]: with per-segment clip R and maxBounces N, every vertex of every path stays within N·R of the camera. In the slab scene above (R_eff ≤ L√3, N = 8) that is about 14 cells in radius, or about 10⁴ instances, within the documented 10⁵–10⁶. This is the exact cross-check that does test pairings.
- Validation rule 'albedo < 1 ceiling' (§3.2, §6.3) is both too strict and not equivalent to finiteness [reasoned]: it would ban mirrors and the project's lossless dielectrics, which have albedo 1. A scene of glass plus a black emitter has finite radiance, because paths terminate at the emitter. The real condition is a spectral radius of T below 1 (for example ‖T^k‖ < 1 for some k), which is not statically checkable. Under the owner's no-guesses rule, a declared, checkable sufficient condition is needed, or the issue is left to the declared maxBounces truncation. Uniforms are the wrong place.
- Per-ray cost and the 'no WebGPU-only obstacle' claim (§0, §4.3, §4.4) [reasoned, unmeasured]: the teleport loop wraps a full scene_intersect (BVH traversal with its stack) and is itself inside the path loop. That is three nested loops, with a root-to-leaf BVH restart per crossing, an NEE shadow ray that also crosses cells, and closed scenes where every path runs until absorbed (mean length about 1/(1−ρ)). The real WebGL2 risks are shader compile time (ANGLE on D3D) and per-draw GPU watchdog and context loss from tail rays, not missing features. They are mitigable with tiling and smaller draws, but should be listed and measured. SwiftShader witness time will multiply; CLAUDE.md already notes minutes per sample for some GRIN NEE scenes.
- 'Cost bounded by far clip ÷ cell size' (§0) omits edge and vertex passages, which §4.3 itself mentions. Near a vertex a ray crosses up to the vertex-link cell count (8 for the cube) in a tiny distance. Under float32 rounding, rays within a rounding band of an edge can be classified 'outside' two faces at once. 'Witness asserts zero cap hits' may not be achievable without a robust exit rule; measure the cap-hit rate before committing to that gate.
- MIS pdf at a BSDF hit in another cell (§2.3) [read: quad.glsl:36-40 computes d = light_p − p in one chart]: light_p and p are in different charts after teleports, so the existing formula is wrong as is. A simpler fix than conjugating by cellBoost is to use the accumulated path length t (the X-distance d(x̃, γỹ)) in the curved-space Jacobian, plus the local emitter cosine at the hit. Then cellBoost is needed only for membership, not for the pdf.
- Light tree (§5, §7) [reasoned]: in E³ quotients the existing light tree is not merely 'not usable'. Build it over the K image lights of a fixed patch (for example the 27-cell patch, with membership by ivec3 table). It then gives an x-adaptive p(γ|x) that can already be evaluated at a BSDF hit. In S³ and H³ its Euclidean bounds do not apply, as stated, but unrolled S³ also needs an S³-aware TLAS. Great-circle rays fail Euclidean AABB slab tests; a gnomonic or Klein-type projective chart, where geodesics are straight lines, is the likely route [missed in §5].
- Free-path tails (§3.1, §9.6) [from memory, not checked]: the tail depends on corridor dimension. Planar (2D) horizons give P(τ > t) ~ 1/t from interior points; if only 1D tube corridors exist, the tail is about 1/t² (Dettmann, J. Stat. Phys. 2012, 'New horizons in multidimensional diffusion', from memory). The Bourgain–Golse–Wennberg 1/t bounds are in the Boltzmann–Grad (small-scatterer) scaling and do not transfer to a fixed-geometry torus. The scene-design advice should be stated in terms of horizons.
- Cusps (§0, §4.3) [reasoned]: at a fixed far clip R, per-ray cost is not unbounded. A geodesic reaching height h up a cusp makes about e^{h} crossings, and h ≤ about R/2, so the worst case is about e^{R/2} and the expected cost is modest. The real issue is the warp tail and the cap, not unboundedness. The recommendation (an authored truncating surface) stands.
- Black-hole cross-link (§2.5) needs a stronger caveat [reasoned]: partition MIS is unbiased only if NEE's pdf is the exact density of the geodesic actually traced and its endpoint is the actual sample. With an approximate two-point solve, the shadow geodesic does not end at y, the flat-style G term is the wrong Jacobian (lensing magnification), and visibility is tested to the wrong point. One unbiased form treats NEE as directional sampling from a proposal with a known pdf (for example a cone around the solver's estimate), traces the real geodesic, and counts it only if it hits the light. The other evaluates the Jacobian dω/dA by integrating Jacobi fields along the traced geodesic, leaving a residual bias of the order of the solver tolerance that must be declared. Image membership ('primary image') must be computed from the traced ray, for example by swept azimuth, the same way on both sides.
- §0 should not say 'feasible' without the qualifier [process]: every load-bearing claim (partition MIS, H³ shadowing, edge robustness, cost) is [reasoned] with no twin test or measurement. Per the owner's rules, the verdict should be 'thought through, not checked; E³/Z³ milestone gated on a TypeScript χ²/pdf twin of image-aware MIS and the estimator-equality witness'.

**Missed approaches**

- Elliptic-space reduction: when −I ∈ Γ, render S³/Γ as RP³/(Γ/±1), with geodesics of length π, one arc per element, and half the table size.
- Projective charts (Klein for H³, gnomonic for S³, where geodesics are straight lines): inside D, faces become Euclidean planes and the existing Euclidean BVH and slab tests work in the cell. This also gives an S³ TLAS for unrolled spherical quotients.
